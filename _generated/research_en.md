## Under review

```{=html}
<article class="paper" id="consolidation">
<h3 class="paper-title">Decomposing the Debt Response to Fiscal Consolidation: Evidence from EU-27 Local Projections, 1996–2024</h3>
<p class="paper-meta">With <a href="https://orcid.org/0000-0001-7009-1567">Cristina Mazas Pérez-Oleaga</a> and <a href="https://portaldelainvestigacion.uma.es/investigadores/1241699/detalle">José M. Domínguez-Martínez</a></p>
<p class="paper-summary">Using local projections difference-in-differences on 125 consolidation entries in the EU-27, we find no evidence that entering a fiscal consolidation lowers the debt-to-GDP ratio within five years. Conditional on the fiscal position at entry, the response is centred on zero; without that conditioning the ratio rises in the short run, consistent with selection. An exact decomposition shows the improvement in the primary balance offset by the snowball term and by stock-flow adjustments, while output contracts, public investment falls and no decline in borrowing costs is detected.</p>
<p class="paper-links"><a href="blog/posts/consolidacion-fiscal-deuda.html">Summary in Spanish</a></p>
</article>
```

## Working papers

```{=html}
<article class="paper" id="feedback">
<h3 class="paper-title">Event-study estimators when adoption responds to past outcomes</h3>
<p class="paper-summary">When units adopt a policy in response to past shocks to the outcome, adopters are selected on bad shocks and the units still untreated on good ones. Under a factorised adoption hazard, the paper derives a closed form for the bias of every calendar-weight event-study estimator, shows that none of them is unbiased under two different persistences of the untreated outcome, and builds joint confidence sets for the dynamics and the effect. Applications to banking deregulation, fiscal rules and democratisation.</p>
</article>
<article class="paper" id="biomarkers">
<h3 class="paper-title">Does Biological Ageing Foreshadow Financial Fragility? Evidence from Dried-Blood-Spot Biomarkers and Household Wealth in Europe</h3>
<p class="paper-summary">Links blood biomarkers collected in SHARE in 2015 to household net wealth six years later, for about 7,400 respondents aged 50 and over in 12 European countries. Conditional on baseline wealth, income and health, a one-standard-deviation higher index of biological risk predicts net wealth about 7% lower; part of that gap reflects persistent economic position rather than later decline.</p>
</article>
```
